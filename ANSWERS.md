# THINGS THAT RECUR ACROSS THE SET (read this first)

Five distinct object vocabularies show up again and again. I name them the same way every time:

- **Striped gantry** — a yellow/black diagonally-striped beam held up on tall yellow legs. The beam sits *above ground level*, roughly head/chest height, with open road underneath. It spans one, two, or occasionally all three lanes.
- **Ground blockers** — parked trucks/buses, stacks of orange pipes, wooden crates, white-and-blue cooler boxes, teal scooters lying on their sides, red-and-white striped roadworks barriers, orange cones, potted plants. Solid, at road level.
- **Green strip** — a dark-green lane covering with bright green forward-pointing chevrons/arrows painted on it. The arrows point away from the camera.
- **Red carpet** — a plain dark-red lane-length covering. Unlike the green strip it carries **no arrows, chevrons, or symbols of any kind**.
- **Gold ovals** — heaps of gold/tan rounded lumps (coins or bread loaves) sitting loose on the road, and occasional single items (a water bottle, fruit baskets). These read as things to collect, not things to avoid.

**On distance, the same answer is true for nearly every frame, so I'll state it once:** there is
**no numeric distance readout anywhere**. What you actually get is (a) the tan dashes on the purple
lane dividers, evenly spaced, which work as a ruler you can count; (b) the streetlamps and white
kerb bollards, also evenly spaced down both sides; (c) apparent size relative to the runner's own
body; (d) height up the screen — higher = further. The overhead **MILE N** signs tell you where you
are in the race, **not** how far away the next obstacle is.

**On faster/slower:** the green strip is the only marking that *states* a direction — its arrows
point the way you're running, which is the standard visual language for "go faster here". The red
carpet is a lane marking too, but **nothing in any frame tells you which way it works**; I refuse to
guess it's a boost just because it's a carpet. I flag it as "singled out, effect unstated" every time.

The runner is in the **MIDDLE lane in every single image** where he's visible.

# THE THREE THINGS MOST WORTH KNOWING

1. **The green strip with forward arrows is the only marking in the set that states a direction.** It appears in the left lane (19a8e753, 89fa4200, 926d0d96, aab292f6, 774496f8), the middle (a262d9f7, and small pads at 2d7c1a2a / 44600860) and the right (8d35a07b, d83541d2, d3d08bb0, d91b15da) — so it is not tied to any fixed lane.

2. **The marked lane is not always the safe lane.** In aab292f6 the green strip runs directly into the back of a parked truck; in 89fa4200 it runs under a barrier beam; in fcb856e9 the red carpet runs into a truck. Treating "marked" as "go here" would put you into an obstacle in at least three frames.

3. **Striped gantry beams and solid ground objects need completely different responses**, and telling them apart is the main perceptual task here. The beams are elevated with open road under them (duck); trucks, crates, pipes, coolers and pots are not (dodge). In 3cc67333, c8cfe625 and c3d36b19 the difference is the whole answer: in c3d36b19 the beam covers all three lanes so no dodge exists, and in c8cfe625 the pots cover all three lanes so no duck exists.
