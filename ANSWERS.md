# Answers, set B

Twelve images, each viewed on its own. All appear to be low-resolution
(≈136×173 px) renders of a stylised road scene, so I upscaled each one to
inspect it. Where the resolution genuinely defeats identification I have said
"cannot tell" rather than dressing up a guess.

Answers are given by filename, in alphabetical order.

---

## 096bdd17.png

1. **What is it?** Cannot tell precisely. A teal/turquoise structure standing
   across the lane: two upright posts at the lane edges, each with a
   handlebar-like crossbar at the top, a round grey disc below it and a dark
   wheel-like shape at the base — they read as the front ends of two
   bikes/scooters — with a solid horizontal teal mass bridging the gap between
   them at about half their height. Best single description: a low teal barrier
   or rail spanning the lane.
2. **Over / under / around?** **Over.**
3. **Confidence:** identification — *guessing*; over/under/around — *fairly sure*.

**Why:** The horizontal member runs from post to post with no gap beneath it —
sampling the pixels straight down the middle of the lane goes from teal
directly to road surface, so there is nothing to duck under — and it is only
about half the height of the flanking posts, i.e. hurdle height. It also fills
the lane edge to edge, so there is no room to pass beside it, and the road
leading up to it is painted with big yellow chevrons pointing forward rather
than the hatched "blocked" marking seen under the other obstacles.

## Note on how I answered

Ten of the twelve images show a vehicle or a works barricade sitting on a
hatched section of road, solid from tarmac to above head height — those are all
"around", and I'm confident about that even where I can't name the exact model
of truck. The two remaining images (`096bdd17.png` and `4f43a1a2.png`) are
different in kind: the obstacle is low, spans the lane so there is no way past
it sideways, has no clearance beneath, and the approach is painted with
forward-pointing chevrons instead of the hatching. That is why those two are
"over" — but I could not identify the object itself with any confidence, and I
have said so rather than inventing a name for it.
