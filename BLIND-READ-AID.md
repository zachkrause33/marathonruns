## What the picture is, in general

Six panels, two rows of three, columns labelled 8u, 12u, 25u — the same moment framed
from three distances. Each panel has a small grey pixel measurement in its bottom-left
corner: top row 86x86px, 65x64px, 36x35px; bottom row 104x75px, 78x55px, 43x29px.
Those read as the on-screen size of the hovering object at each distance. A grey "1"
sits in the left margin of each row.

The scene is a view down a city street, low and close behind a character — the bottom
of every panel is filled by the back of a head: a red domed cap with a gold band and
dark hair under it. The road is dark asphalt running to a blown-out white vanishing
point. Pale grey lines run the length of the road in two pairs, which I read as lane
paint but could equally be tram rails. Kerbs and pavements on both sides carry
red-and-white striped barrier fencing, ranks of dark lamp posts and street furniture,
and small blocky spectators in saturated greens, pinks, yellows and blues. Behind them
are purple, teal and blue building faces and hoardings. In the 25u panels a dark
overhead gantry or footbridge crosses the street. The light is flat and bright ahead,
hazy at the horizon.

## (a) The small hovering object

**Top row: a drink cup, or a small bottle.** At 8u it is a pale, roughly cylindrical
object standing upright just off the road, white or very pale in the body with a
mint-green band at the top that reads as a lid or a rim, and a second green band at the
bottom. It is about as tall as it is wide (86x86px), which is what pushes me to "cup
with a lid" rather than "bottle" — a bottle would be taller than wide. It has a soft
pale glow around it.

Can I name it from the 25u panel alone? **No, not honestly.** At 36x35px it is a small
pale smudge with a greenish cast, sitting above a green ring. I could tell you
something small and light-coloured is floating there. I could not tell you it is a cup.
If I had never seen the 8u panel I would have said "a pale pickup of some kind" and
stopped. And I have to be honest that "cup" is the reading a marathon aid station would
predict, and I already knew the word "aid".

**Bottom row: a banana.** At 8u it is unmistakable — a yellow crescent lying on its
side, wider than tall (104x75px), tapering at both ends with a darker brown-ish tip at
one end, and a paler yellow highlight along the upper edge. Nothing else is that shape
and that colour.

Can I name it from the 25u panel alone? **Probably yes, though with less confidence
than at 8u.** At 43x29px it is still a recognisably curved yellow sliver against dark
asphalt. The crescent silhouette survives the shrink better than the cup's silhouette
does, and yellow against near-black is a stronger separation than pale-green against a
pale glow. I would have said "banana" and I think I would have been right. It is a
guess made from about a dozen pixels of shape, but it is a guess I would stand behind.

Comparing the two: **the banana reads at distance and the cup does not.** The cup loses
because its colour is pale and desaturated in a panel that already has bright pale
haze, and because its shape is a plain blob. The banana keeps a distinctive outline and
a colour nothing else in frame owns.

## (b) The coloured markings on the road

**1. Long violet streaks.** The most visually dominant thing on the road surface, in
every panel. They are elongated soft-edged parallelograms of saturated purple lying
flat along the road direction, several of them, spread across the lanes at different
depths, brighter and more opaque at the near end and fading with distance. They do not
touch the hovering object or line up with it.

I cannot identify these with confidence. My competing readings, in order:
- **Coloured light cast on the road** — spill from purple neon signage, shopfronts or
  hoardings on the left side, projected onto the asphalt. The soft edges and the
  along-the-road angle fit this.
- **Painted speed or boost strips** — a gameplay lane marking the player runs over.
  The regular repeating placement fits this.
- **Tinted shadows** — shadows of tall objects thrown at a low sun angle, coloured by
  a purple sky or bounce light. The consistent single direction fits this.

I lean toward light spill over paint, because the near ends look brighter than the far
ends in a way paint would not, but I would not bet much on it.

**2. A glowing green ellipse.** Directly on the road below and slightly ahead of the
hovering object, in every panel of both rows: a mint-green oval ring with a paler,
almost white centre, sitting flat on the asphalt. It is clearly a marker tied to the
object — a ground indicator saying "the pickup is here", a shadow-substitute, or a
landing/collect footprint. Its colour is the same green family as the cup's bands,
which is why the cup blends into it and the banana does not.

**3. Lane paint.** Continuous pale grey lines running the length of the road in two
long pairs, dividing the surface into lanes.

**4. Short cream dashes.** Small stubby tan-cream rectangles at regular intervals
between the lanes, offset from the continuous lines. Standard lane dashes, or possibly
small flat mats laid in the road — they are slightly warmer and slightly wider than the
lane paint.

**5. A broad pale cream band across the road.** Visible in the bottom-left 8u panel,
spanning the full width of the road perpendicular to travel, close to the camera. It is
much wider than the dashes. I read it as a painted crossing, a timing mat or a
start/split line. Only the near panel is close enough to show it.

## (c) What the eye goes to first at 25u — the honest ranking

In the 25u panels, in the order my eye actually lands:

1. **The blown-out white vanishing point**, dead centre, framed by the dark gantry. It
   is the brightest thing in the frame by a wide margin and the road's perspective
   lines all point at it. It gets the first fixation for free.
2. **The violet streaks on the road.** These are the loudest deliberate thing in the
   picture. They are large, saturated, they sit on the darkest surface in frame so the
   contrast is enormous, and there are several of them, so they occupy more total area
   than anything else on the road. My eye goes here second and lingers.
3. **The red cap of the runner** at the bottom edge — a hard saturated red against
   dark, though it is cropped and static so it releases attention quickly.
4. **The green ellipse on the road** under the object.
5. **The busy spectator colour** along both pavements — pinks, greens and yellows in
   small blocks. Not any single one of them, but the crowded texture pulls.
6. **The hovering object itself** — the cup or the banana.

So the answer to what you actually asked: **yes, things on the road out-shout the
hovering object at 25u, clearly and by a lot.** The violet streaks beat it decisively —
they are bigger, more saturated, higher contrast against their background, and there
are several of them competing against one small item. The green ellipse also beats the
object it is meant to point at, which is the more awkward of the two: the marker is
brighter and larger than the thing it marks, so at distance you see the ring first and
only then look up to find what it belongs to. In the top row that is compounded, because
the cup shares the ring's green and pale value, so the two merge into one soft blob
rather than reading as marker-plus-object.

The bottom row survives this better. The banana still loses the ranking, but it holds
its own identity once found, because its yellow belongs to nothing else on the road and
its crescent is unlike every other shape in frame. The cup at 25u does not survive being
found — I locate it and still cannot say what it is.

One caveat on all of the above: I am ranking from a static image of a static frame. If
the streaks and the object move differently in motion, or if the streaks are lighting
that shifts while the pickup stays put, motion could re-sort this ranking completely,
and I have no way to tell from one picture.
