# Blind read

## Disclosure first

I did not open, list, grep or search anything except the four PNGs named in the
task. No source, no docs, no directory listings, no git history, no web.

But I have to declare contamination, because two things were already in front of
me before I started and I cannot un-know them:

1. **The working directory is called `marathonruns`.** That word was in my
   environment description.
2. **A project instruction file was auto-injected into my context by the
   harness before your message arrived.** I did not go and read it. It appeared
   on its own. It is a rules document about how to build things, and from it I
   involuntarily learned roughly this: that this is a game; that it has
   obstacles, vehicles, buildings, trees, crowds, marshals, signs, banners, a
   ghost and a runner; that there is a chase camera behind the player; that
   hazards have to be visible against the road; and that lane dashes and road
   paint are flat markings.

That is real contamination and it is not small. In particular, "marshal",
"runner", "chase camera" and "hazard" are words I would otherwise have had to
arrive at myself, and where I use them below you should discount them. Where I
think a reading is genuinely coming from the pixels rather than from that
leaked vocabulary I say so. Everything else in this document is only what I can
see.

I have not looked at anything that would tell me what any specific one of these
objects is meant to be, which is the actual question, so I think the test still
has value — just less than it would have had.

A note on what the four files are as images: each is a contact sheet on a dark
grey background. `figure-v3`, `figure-v8` and `figure-v9` are two panels each,
captioned `8u` and `12u`, with a version tag (`v3`, `v8`, `v9`) in the left
margin and a size stamp under each panel (e.g. `104x124  8534px`). `aid.png` is
six panels, two rows of three, columns captioned `8u`, `12u`, `25u`, both rows
tagged `1`. So `u` is plainly a distance and the stamp is how many pixels the
subject covers at that distance — these are legibility-at-range tests. I am
reading the `8u` panel in each case and then asking what survives at `12u` and
`25u`.

One thing is common to all four files and I will describe it once. At the bottom
centre of nearly every panel there is a large rounded dome: a red/crimson cap of
colour on top, a thick gold-yellow band around it, and a dark navy-black mass
below. In the bottom-left panel of `aid.png` it is bigger and I can see more of
it — under the gold band there is dark hair, and below that a strip of blue.
That resolves it: it is the back of a person's head and shoulders, very close to
the camera, wearing a red cap or hood with a gold band, seen from behind. So all
of these are over-the-shoulder views following someone. I am confident of that
from the pixels alone; the bottom-left `aid` panel gives it away without needing
any outside knowledge.

The road in `figure-v3/v8/v9` is a wide multi-lane highway running dead ahead to
a white haze on the horizon, with lamp posts and heavy tree cover both sides, a
kerb and barrier line, and an overhead gantry carrying a pale rectangular board.
The road in `aid.png` is a much narrower urban street: buildings close on both
sides, red-and-white striped barrier fencing running along both kerbs, a lot of
small figures behind the fencing, and overhead structures spanning the street.

## figure-v3.png

### (a) The people

There are two figures, plus the head at the bottom of frame.

They stand one either side of the road, flanking a barrier that blocks the
middle of the carriageway. Both are upright and static. Both have dark
navy/black bodies which read as a uniform rather than as casual clothing,
because there is no colour variation between torso and legs — a single dark
block from shoulder to shoe.

**Left figure.** Dark from head to foot, with a small pale face and what looks
like a peaked or brimmed dark cap. There is a lighter vertical accent down the
front which could be a zip, a strap, or a lanyard. There is something narrow
held or worn at the side. My reading: someone official stationed at a closure —
a police officer or a traffic officer. The cues are the flat dark uniform, the
cap, the fixed stance, and above all the company they keep, which is a barricade
and cones.

**Right figure.** Same dark body but with two extra pieces of colour: a bright
red or hot-pink block where the head is, and a broad white/pale panel across the
chest. The red could be a helmet, a cap, or hair. The white could be a shirt
front, a sash worn diagonally, or a hi-vis panel that has gone white rather than
yellow. My reading: a second official of a different kind — a works operative or
a steward rather than police, because of the helmet-coloured head and the chest
panel. If I had to give the single competing reading: this could equally be one
person in a red bike helmet, i.e. someone who arrived on a motorbike, since the
red mass sits high and hard-edged like a helmet rather than soft like hair.

I want to be honest about the confidence here. At `8u` each figure is about 25
pixels tall. What I am actually reading is "dark upright thing, human
proportioned, with a red spot on top" and "dark upright thing with a pale
spot on top". The *role* I am assigning comes almost entirely from the props
around them, not from the figures themselves. Take the barricade and cones away
and I could not tell you these were officials at all — only that they were two
people standing still.

At `12u` (79x94, about 4,900 px of subject) both figures survive as presences
but nothing about them survives as detail. The red head stays legible; the left
figure very nearly vanishes into the dark of the barrier and the trees behind
it. If the question of this contact sheet is "does the person still read at
12u", my answer is: you can see that *someone is there*, but which someone is
gone. The red is doing all the work.

### (b) Things I cannot identify

- **The red disc on a pole**, standing between the two figures at head height.
  Flat, circular, strong red, with what may be a pale horizontal bar across it.
  Competing readings: a no-entry sign (red disc, white bar — this is the closest
  match and the bar is what sells it); a plain stop/closed sign; or a hand-held
  lollipop sign carried by one of the figures rather than planted in the ground.
  I cannot see the bottom of the pole clearly enough to say whether it is
  planted or held, and that is exactly the ambiguity.
- **The main barrier.** A wide horizontal board with bold red-and-white
  diagonal stripes, standing on thin pale legs, spanning the middle lanes. That
  much is clear and I am not puzzled by it — it is a road-closure barricade.
  What I am unsure of is its **width relative to the road**: it does not span
  the full carriageway, and there appears to be passable road either side of it.
  So I cannot tell whether it means "road closed" or "keep left/right".
- **The cones.** Four orange-and-white traffic cones at the barrier's base, two
  each side, angled outward. Straightforward. I mention them only because
  they are drawn leaning rather than upright, which reads as either
  deliberately knocked-about or just a stylisation.
- **A dark shape immediately behind and above the barrier**, roughly where a
  vehicle's cab would be. I genuinely cannot tell whether that is a vehicle
  parked behind the closure, the shadowed rear of the barrier itself, or just
  the tree line showing through the gap. Three readings, no way to choose.
- **The pale rectangle on the overhead gantry.** Blank at this resolution. A
  matrix sign, a direction board, or a banner. Nothing on it reads.
- **A tall flat blue slab on the right, beyond the kerb.** Reads as a building
  face or a hoarding/billboard seen edge-on. It is featureless.
- **Rows of short pale posts with dark blue tops** along both verges. Bollards,
  or the tops of a low barrier fence. They are evenly spaced and are probably
  just roadside furniture.
- **Dark bands running across the road at intervals**, perpendicular to travel.
  Either shadows cast by the lamp posts, or transverse joints/rumble strips in
  the surface. I lean shadows, because their spacing matches the lamp spacing,
  but I cannot rule out surface detail.
- **A short yellow-tan dash in the left lane**, a different colour from the
  white lane dashes. Some kind of secondary marking. No idea what it denotes.
- **The white haze filling the far end of the road.** Fog, glare, or a fade-out.
  It sits exactly where the vanishing point is and I would guess it is a
  deliberate distance fade rather than weather.

## figure-v8.png

### (a) The people

Two figures, standing side by side, close together, right in the middle of the
running surface — not at the verge. They are roughly the same height and they
face the camera.

**Left figure.** A solid amber/yellow block for the torso — bright, saturated,
and clearly a garment rather than skin. A small dark head above it. Below the
torso the legs are dark with two pale vertical stripes down them. Those stripes
are the interesting bit and I read them two ways: either they are reflective
bands on dark trousers, or the "dark" is a gap and I am seeing two pale bare
legs against the shadow under the body. I lean towards reflective bands,
because the pale runs the full length rather than stopping at an ankle.

**Right figure.** Torso in red/magenta with a white patch on it, dark legs, and
a small light-coloured head. Slightly narrower than its neighbour.

My reading of both: people in high-visibility clothing, standing on the road,
positioned deliberately as a pair. The saturated amber against the grey road is
the classic hi-vis read and I think that one is coming from the pixels rather
than from the leaked vocabulary. What they are *doing* I cannot tell. Two
competing readings: they are stationed there (stewards, marshals, workers at a
point on the route), or they are simply two people using the road who happen to
be dressed brightly. Nothing here — no barrier, no cones, no sign held — settles
it, which is a real difference from v3 where the props told the whole story.

There is a small red/pink form directly above the right figure's shoulder. That
could be a raised arm, a flag, a held object, or an antenna. I cannot resolve it.

At `12u` (50x89, 2,434 px) the pair collapses into a single two-tone blob:
yellow on the left, red on the right, dark underneath. You can still tell there
are *two* of them, and only because the two colours are different. If they had
both been amber they would have merged into one shape. That feels like the point
of the test and it is the clearest legibility finding I have across the three
figure sheets.

### (b) Things I cannot identify

- **A pale strip with faint red-and-white diagonal stripes lying flat on the
  road at their feet**, spanning roughly the width of the pair. Because it lies
  flat and is foreshortened, it is a marking or a mat, not a standing board.
  Competing readings: a timing mat; a painted band across the road; a strip of
  hazard tape; a low ramp or threshold. The diagonal stripes make me want to say
  hazard/warning tape, but the fact it is under their feet rather than in front
  of them argues for something you cross rather than something you avoid.
- **A small blue diamond sign on a post** at the left verge, and a matching one
  further back. Diamond-on-post is a distinctive sign shape but the face is
  blank at this size. Could be a route marker, a hazard warning, or a distance
  marker.
- **Whether the two figures are touching or overlapping.** Their torsos abut
  with no gap. I cannot tell if they are shoulder to shoulder or if one is
  slightly behind the other, which matters for reading them as a pair versus as
  two independent things that happen to be in line.
- Same unresolved background items as v3: the blank gantry board, the blue slab
  to the right, the bollard rows, the transverse shadow bands, the white haze
  at the vanishing point.

## figure-v9.png

### (a) The people

This is the one I am least sure about, and I will give you the uncertainty
rather than a confident answer.

The dominant object is a tall boxy mass in the middle of the lane, seen from
directly behind. It is red-orange, with a horizontal gold/yellow band along its
top edge, a broader crimson section above that, and a bright white-and-red
diagonally striped panel across its lower rear. It is wider than a person and
noticeably taller than the figures in v8.

Beneath it and slightly forward of it there is a small figure in red/pink, low
down, centred, with what look like two arms out to the sides at a width that
suggests handlebars, and something dark and wheel-like directly under it.

**My primary reading:** this is one person riding a two-wheeler — a motorcycle
or scooter — carrying a very large box or load behind and above them. The rider
is the small red figure; the box is the mass above. What sells it is the low
seated posture, the arms out to a fixed width, and the fact that the whole
assembly is narrow enough to sit in one lane.

**My competing reading:** this is a small truck or three-wheeler seen from
behind, the striped panel is a chevron board on its tailgate, and the small red
figure at the bottom is not a rider at all but a separate person standing behind
the vehicle — or simply the vehicle's rear lights and number plate area which I
am pattern-matching into a person.

**A third possibility** I cannot dismiss: there are two people, not one. The
darker crimson form at the very top of the mass has a shape that could be a
head and shoulders above the load — a pillion passenger, or a person sitting on
top of the box.

So: somewhere between one and two people, most likely one, riding something.
That is as far as the pixels take me. At `12u` (46x92, 2,719 px) the box and its
striped panel remain perfectly legible and the rider figure is the first thing
to go — the person is the least durable part of this composition, which is the
opposite of v8.

### (b) Things I cannot identify

- **The white-and-red diagonal striped panel** on the lower rear. Two readings,
  and they are quite different: a warning chevron board of the kind fitted to
  slow or working vehicles, or the open rear face of a load bed with a striped
  tailgate. The stripes are the same red/white diagonal motif as the barrier in
  v3 and the flat strip in v8, so the same visual idiom is being reused for
  three different things across these three pictures, and that is itself
  slightly confusing as a viewer.
- **The gold/yellow band along the top.** A beacon or light bar, the top edge
  trim of the box, or a roof rack. I cannot tell if it is emitting or reflecting.
- **The dark crimson mass at the very top.** Head and shoulders of a second
  person, the raised lid of the box, or a further section of load. Genuinely
  ambiguous.
- **What the thing is resting on.** I can see something dark and round low and
  centre, which I want to call a rear wheel, but I cannot see a second wheel or
  any axle, so I cannot confirm the two-wheeler reading from the running gear.
- **The blue diamond signs on posts** at the left verge again, unreadable.
- Background items as before: gantry board, blue slab, bollards, haze.

## aid.png

### The hovering object

**Top row (all three panels): a bottle.** Pale translucent green-white body,
slightly waisted, with a narrow neck and a small bright cap on top, and a
lighter band or label around the middle. It reads to me as a plastic drinks
bottle — water or a sports drink. The give-away is the neck-and-cap silhouette;
almost nothing else has that profile. It hangs a little above the road surface
rather than sitting on it.

**Bottom row (all three panels): a banana.** A yellow crescent, thicker in the
middle, tapering at both ends, with a small dark brown nub at one tip which
reads as the stem. Curved the right way, coloured the right way, proportioned
the right way. I am as confident about this as I am about anything in the four
files. It also hovers slightly above the road.

Legibility across the range, since that is what the sheet is asking: at `8u`
both are unmistakable. At `12u` both still read — the bottle keeps its cap and
neck, the banana keeps its curve. At `25u` (36x35 and 43x29 px of subject) they
both degrade badly. The bottle becomes a small pale-green vertical smudge and I
would not be able to name it from that alone. The banana at `25u` becomes a
yellow horizontal smear; the curve is just barely there and I think I am reading
it partly from having seen the larger panels first, which means it probably does
not stand alone. At that range, in both rows, the thing that actually tells you
something is present is not the object at all — it is the green marking under
it, which stays crisp and stays the same size relative to the road.

### The coloured markings on the road

There are two quite different kinds and I am confident they are unrelated to
each other.

**The green ellipse.** Directly beneath each hovering object there is a bright
mint-green oval lying flat on the road, brighter than the road around it, with a
soft edge and a paler core. It tracks the object exactly — same lane, same
position, present in all six panels. Readings, in the order I believe them:

1. A marker or pad that says "the thing is here", drawn on the ground so that
   the object's position in the lane is unambiguous even when the object itself
   is too small or too high to place. This is my strong preference, because it
   is *brighter* than the road and because it survives distance better than the
   object does.
2. A shadow, stylised as a coloured glow rather than a darkening. Possible, and
   the placement is exactly where a shadow would be — but shadows go darker,
   and this goes lighter, so I do not believe it.
3. A separate collectible or zone that the object happens to sit above. I
   mention it for completeness but the one-to-one pairing across all six panels
   argues against two independent things.

**The purple streaks.** Long violet-purple diagonal dashes lying flat on the
road surface, several per panel, in more than one lane, all at the same angle,
repeating down the street at regular intervals. These I cannot resolve and I
will give you all three readings:

1. **Light, not paint.** Coloured light or shadow cast onto the road by the
   structures overhead. In favour: they are all parallel, they all lean the same
   way, their spacing down the street matches the spacing of the overhead
   gantries and lamp standards, and there is purple/magenta colour on the
   hanging banners above, which is where such light would come from. This is
   my leading reading.
2. **Paint.** Diagonal markings applied to the road. In favour: they are
   crisp-edged and uniformly saturated, which light usually is not. Against:
   real road paint is very rarely purple, and paint would not usually be laid
   in this repeating diagonal rhythm across multiple lanes.
3. **Motion or speed streaks** — a rendering effect rather than anything in the
   world. Against: they are fixed in the scene and do not vary with the panels.

I want to flag that at `25u`, top row, the purple streaks are visually the
*loudest* thing in the frame — considerably louder than the pale bottle they
share the road with. Whatever they are, they compete with the small object for
attention at long range, and the green ellipse is the only reason the object
still reads.

**Other markings**, which I think are ordinary: long pale continuous lines
separating the lanes; short yellow-tan dashes in the left lane, the same colour
as the odd dash I noticed in `figure-v3`, so probably the same kind of marking
in both files; and a broad light-grey band along the left edge which reads as a
hard shoulder or a paved margin rather than a marking.

### Other things in aid.png I cannot identify

- **The red-and-white striped fencing** running the full length of both kerbs
  in every panel. Crowd-control barriers, near-certainly.
- **The many small figures behind that fencing**, several deep in places, in
  assorted bright colours. People watching from behind the barriers. At this
  size they are two or three pixels each and are pure colour noise; I can tell
  there is a crowd, I cannot tell you a single thing about any individual in it.
- **Structures spanning overhead** carrying what look like hanging banners or
  planting in magenta and green. Could be street decoration, signage, or
  vegetation. Cannot tell.
- **In the bottom-left panel only**, the back of the head at the bottom of frame
  is larger and shows dark hair under the gold-banded red cap plus a blue
  shoulder line. As I said at the top, this is what resolves the dome shape in
  all the other panels.
- **A red-orange shape at the bottom-right of the top-right panel**, cut off by
  the frame. Probably the same head, cropped differently because the framing
  differs. Not certain.

## What I would say overall

The three `figure-*` sheets are not equally legible and they fail in different
places. v3's people are the least legible of the three at `8u` and depend
entirely on their props for meaning; take the barrier and cones away and they
are two dark smudges. v8's pair is the most legible as *people* and the least
legible as *purpose* — I can see them clearly and cannot tell you why they are
standing there. v9 is the reverse of v8: the object is completely durable at
range and the person on it is the first thing to disappear.

Across all four files the single most reliable carrier of meaning at distance is
saturated hue against the grey road — the amber torso, the red head, the yellow
banana, the green ellipse. Silhouette and detail are gone by `12u`. Colour is
still working at `25u`, and only just.
