# Blind identification, after

What I see in each picture, judged only from the pixels.

## figure-v2.png

One person, sitting down, driving a small open utility vehicle — the sort of
low-speed buggy or works cart a course crew uses, green bodywork with an orange
flatbed and a bright red-and-white chevron panel across the tail. They are seen
from directly behind: a pinkish-red flat-topped cap, a dark navy jacket or
long-sleeved top, and light tan skin at the neck and side of the face. The seat
back and a second dark navy block sit to their right; I cannot tell whether that
is a passenger with their head out of frame or just the empty other half of the
bench, so I would not swear there is only one person aboard. What makes me call
them a course worker rather than an ordinary driver is entirely the vehicle —
hazard stripes, roll bar, flashing-lamp look to the round red fittings — not
anything they are wearing. Nothing on the figure itself says "official": no
tabard, no high-vis, no lettering.

## figure-v3.png

Two people standing shoulder to shoulder behind a striped road barrier with
traffic cones at either end, and they read clearly as race marshals or traffic
stewards. Both wear the same uniform — a pale grey short-sleeved shirt or tabard
over a navy long-sleeve, dark trousers and matching red flat caps, which is what
makes it look like a uniform rather than two people who happened to dress alike.
The left one has darker brown skin and is holding up a round red-and-white sign
on a stick, the international "no entry" disc, held out at head height the way a
lollipop man or point-duty officer holds it. The right one has lighter skin and
has both arms down and resting on the barrier top, standing squarely in the way.
Between them at waist height is a small pale green object I cannot identify — it
might be a bottle or a flag furled on a pole; it just looks like a green block to
me.

## figure-v8.png

Two cyclists on bicycles, seen from behind, riding side by side across the lane.
Both wear pinkish-red helmets and dark cycling shorts over bare legs; the nearer
one has a mustard-yellow jersey with a red stripe down the back, the farther one
a red jersey. Their bikes have red frames, blue-grey wheels and yellow saddle
areas. I read them as ride-along support or marshal cyclists moving with the race
rather than commuters, mainly because they are matched, they are riding abreast
in the middle of a closed carriageway, and there is a striped hazard mat painted
on the road right under them. That said, nothing on either rider actually says
"official" — no numbers, no high-vis, no bag or radio — so "support cyclists" is
an inference from the setting, not from the clothing. There is a small green
block on the road between them I cannot make out.

## figure-v9.png

One rider on a motorcycle, seen from directly behind, and this one looks like an
escort or lead motorbike. The rider wears a full-face helmet in the same pinkish
red as the bike, with a black visor band across it, and a mustard-yellow jacket
with square yellow panniers or hard boxes on either side at shoulder height. The
bike itself is red and pink with a large yellow-topped top box behind the rider,
a red-and-white chevron panel on the back of that box, gold-rimmed round lamps
either side of the tail, and a yellow bag low on the left. The chevrons and the
lamps are what push me toward "official escort" rather than "delivery rider",
though a courier with a big top box would honestly look much the same from here;
I cannot see any badge, lettering or livery to settle it.

## scene-crowd.png

The runner in the foreground is plainly a marathon competitor: seen from behind
and slightly above, dark hair under a red cap with a gold band, a red-sleeved
running top, and a navy race bib on their back printed 26.2, with chunky white
trainers with gold heel flashes. Far up the road are two more cyclists in yellow
and red jerseys with red helmets — the same pair as the cyclists picture — plus a
faint translucent blue runner-shaped figure over on the left that I take to be a
ghost or pacer marker rather than a real person in the world. Along the right-
hand kerb, behind red-and-white crowd barriers, is a line of spectators in bright
mixed clothing, one of them holding a small yellow placard up over their head;
nearer the camera a man in a mustard shirt and brown trousers stands behind a
green-skirted trestle table covered in rows of small pale cups, which makes him a
drinks-station volunteer handing out water, with a second person in the same
mustard shirt beside him and a dropped cup on the pavement in front. Off to the
left, well back from the barriers on the far pavement, a man in a brown jacket,
blue jeans and blond hair is walking across with his back to the race — he reads
as an ordinary passer-by going about his day rather than a spectator.
