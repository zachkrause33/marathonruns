# Blind identification, before

What I could tell about each person from the pixels alone, with no source, no
docs and no other files read. Recorded as I first judged them.

## figure-v2.png

There is one figure, sitting on a lime-green flatbed cart or buggy with an
orange bench seat, chevron-striped tailgate and two lime uprights. I cannot
tell what kind of person it is. What I actually see is a stack of blocks: a
dark navy slab for the body, a tan cube above it for a head with no face, and a
flat magenta-pink plate laid on top of that cube. The pink plate might be a cap
or a hard hat, but it reads more like a lid than headgear, and nothing else on
the figure — no arms, no hands on a wheel, no vest, nothing held — tells me
what they do. If I had to guess from the vehicle rather than the figure, I would
say the driver of a course maintenance or utility cart, but that is the cart
talking, not the person.

## figure-v3.png

Two figures side by side on a flat trailer with a red-and-white striped board
across the front and orange traffic cones at the corners. Each is built the same
way: a crimson slab over a tan box for the head, a broad pale grey-white band
across the chest, navy below, and small brown cubes out at the sides where hands
would be. Behind the left one is a large red disc with a horizontal grey bar
that I read as a road sign — a no-entry or no-thoroughfare sign. On that basis I
would call them roadworks or course crew standing on a barrier trailer, but I am
going mostly on the cones, the striped board and the sign. Stripped of the props,
the two bodies themselves are just banded blocks and I could equally believe
they were spectators on a viewing stand. The pale chest band could be a hi-vis
sash or could be nothing.

## figure-v8.png

Two people on motorcycles, and this one I am fairly confident about. The bikes
are legible — wheels, forks, a round headlamp each, handlebar stubs. The riders
sit astride them with dark trousers and dark boots. The left rider wears a
mustard-yellow jacket, the right a red jacket with a mustard panel on the back;
both have a tan head cube with a crimson slab on top that I take for a helmet,
though it is the same flat plate shape as in the other images. The right rider
has a brown box behind the shoulder — a backpack or a pannier. I would call them
escort or outrider motorcyclists running ahead of the race, or possibly
couriers. Riders on bikes, certainly; which service they belong to, I cannot
tell, because there is no marking, no insignia and no visible equipment beyond
the box.

## figure-v9.png

One rider on a larger motorcycle, and this is the clearest figure of the four.
The bike is pink-magenta with a white-and-red striped panel on the back, a big
yellow luggage rack or top box, yellow side panniers, a headlamp and two gold
stalks that read as indicators or mirrors. The rider wears a gold-yellow jacket
with dark shoulders, and — unlike every other figure here — the head is an
actual helmet: a rounded red-pink shell with a black horizontal visor band
across it. That visor is what makes it read as a person on a bike rather than a
stack of boxes. I would say a police or official escort motorcyclist, or a
delivery rider, on the strength of the top box and panniers. The colours are too
unusual for me to place the service.

## scene-crowd.png

The runner in the foreground is unmistakable: a marathon runner, seen from
behind and above, mid-stride. Red cap or beanie with a yellow band, black hair
under it, bare tanned arms, a navy singlet with red shoulder panels, a white
race bib reading 26.2, and white running shoes with yellow soles, one lifted so
the sole faces the camera. No doubt at all about this one.

On the left pavement are two pedestrians walking away — blocky figures with dark
hair, one in a purple top and one in a brown coat, both in navy trousers, and one
carrying a small pale rectangle that could be a bag or a sheet of paper. They
read as ordinary passers-by or commuters, not race staff. Further along the same
side, behind the barriers, is a dense packed row of spectators in pink, lilac,
green, blue and white tops with dark legs; several have an arm raised holding a
stick topped with a coloured rectangle, which I read as placards or cheer signs.
Those are crowd, clearly, and clearly cheering. On the right, past the barrier
line, there is a green-skirted table with rows of small white cups on it — an
aid or water station — and next to it a group of tall brown vertical slabs I
genuinely cannot identify; they might be people in brown, or wooden posts, or
market stall frames. Up the road ahead are the two yellow-and-red motorcyclists
from the other image, and beside them a semi-transparent blue figure running,
which I take to be a ghost or pacer runner rather than a real person in the
scene.
