# Blind read: redraft street frames

Written from the two frames alone (`redraft-a.png`, `redraft-b.png`). I have
not looked at any other file in this repository, so this is what a stranger
actually sees, not what the scene is supposed to be.

## Frame A (portrait, `redraft-a.png`)

**What stands beside the road.** A lot, and it is legible. Nearest the runner
on the left there is a red-and-white striped roadwork barricade on legs, and a
yellow traffic-light pole with a full three-lamp head. On the right, three
terracotta pots with green plants sit in a row on the green cycle/route lane.
Both kerbs carry a continuous run of crowd-control barriers — grey posts with a
red top rail — for the whole visible distance. Tall double-headed street lamps
arch in from both sides and converge to the horizon, which sells the depth of
the street better than anything else in the frame. There are hints of a small
crowd far down the right pavement and something blue-and-white in the middle
distance of the road (a vehicle or sign cluster — too far to say).

**The buildings.** Two continuous terraces of three-to-four storey blocks,
mostly beige/tan with dark blue window strips, broken up with occasional red
and brown façades and coloured shopfront awnings at street level. They read as
generic European-city terraces. They are clearly repeated units — the same
window rhythm recurs down the whole street — but at this distance the
repetition reads as "a street", not as copy-paste. The far end dissolves into
haze, which is a kind gradient but also hides whatever the street ends in.

**The road surface.** Mid-grey asphalt, slightly darker than the pavements,
with white dashed lane lines and, in the foreground, wide white transverse
stripes like a crossing or start grid. A green painted lane with big painted
chevron arrows runs up the right side. All markings are flat paint, correctly
so, and the arrows do a lot of work pointing the eye up the course.

**What looks flat, hollow, or fake.** The plants in the pots are the weakest
things in the frame — the greenery is a small blob that reads more like a
scoop of guacamole than foliage. The huge foreground chevron on the right is
so large and so saturated it looks like UI pasted onto the road rather than
paint on it. The building windows are pure flat colour with no depth, glint,
or interior — acceptable at this distance, but the nearest façades are close
enough that the flatness is noticeable. The white transverse stripes are so
bright and clean against the asphalt that the foreground looks freshly
stickered rather than worn. Nothing reads as hollow — the barricade, the
traffic light, and the barriers all look like objects with sides.

**Place and time.** A tidy, toy-like city high street closed for a race —
mid-morning feel, flat bright light, no long shadows. Cheerful rather than
atmospheric; nothing threatening in sight, which makes the barricade the
obvious "thing to deal with".

## Frame B (widescreen, `redraft-b.png`)

**What stands beside the road.** This frame is much busier and better for it.
The right pavement holds a genuine crowd — dozens of blocky spectators, several
with raised square placards in pink, blue and yellow — behind the red-railed
barriers. The left pavement is oddly empty by comparison: barriers, a couple of
dark spherical bushes, a bin or bollard, and what might be a grey utility box,
but no people, so the street feels lopsided, like everyone was told to stand on
one side. Ahead in the runner's lane there is a proper obstacle cluster: a
stack of orange pipes on the green lane and wooden crates beside it. Beyond
that, a big blue overhead gantry spans the whole road, with small figures
standing on top of it, and a yellow structure (scaffold or gate) further on. A
"3" sign hangs in the far distance — a mile or zone marker, presumably. A pale
tower with a bulbous top — very CN-Tower-like — pokes above the skyline, and
glassy blue high-rises close the right background.

**The buildings.** Same terrace language as Frame A — beige and brown blocks,
dark blue windows, coloured awnings — but seen from a lower, wider angle, and
here the repetition is more exposed: the same façade module marches down both
sides with the same awning cadence. The corner building on the far right and
the modern towers behind break it up. The terraces have visible depth at their
rooflines and corners; they do not read as false fronts.

**The road surface.** Light grey asphalt with dashed white lane lines, a white
transverse band mid-distance, and the green painted lane running up the middle
of the frame with chevrons in it. One chevron near the runner has a visibly
jagged, stair-stepped edge — it looks lower-resolution than everything around
it, like a decal that missed anti-aliasing. The kerbs are chunky and read
almost knee-high, more like a platform edge than a kerb.

**What looks flat, hollow, or fake.** The crowd is the biggest offender: the
spectators are colored blocks with heads, which is fine as a style, but many of
them appear to share only a handful of poses, and the placards are blank —
solid colour rectangles with nothing on them, which reads as unfinished rather
than stylised. The blank grey wall under the HUD on the near-left building is
a large featureless slab at the closest viewing distance in the frame. The
jagged chevron edge noted above. The left/right crowd asymmetry makes the left
side feel like a stage that hasn't been dressed. The obstacles themselves —
pipes and crates — look solid and volumetric; the pipe stack even shows the
open ends of the pipes, which is a nice touch of real geometry.

**Place and time.** A closed-off downtown street mid-race, near a landmark
tower — reads as a cartoon Toronto. Daytime again, blue sky with soft clouds,
the same even shadowless light as Frame A. The crowd, gantry, and "3" marker
make it feel like an event in progress rather than an empty set, which Frame A
only manages at a distance.

## Across both frames

The street is convincing as a *race circuit* and less convincing as a *city*:
everything that belongs to the event (barriers, gantries, markings, obstacles)
is confident and dimensional, while everything that belongs to the world
(windows, plants, placards, the undressed left pavement in B) is where the
flatness shows. Light is even and shadowless in both frames, which keeps
everything readable but makes nothing feel anchored to the ground.
